## Research Methodology

The research process used throughout this portfolio includes:

1. Define the research question and required data points.
2. Identify authoritative, relevant, and current sources.
3. Match each data field to the source best qualified to support it.
4. Preserve the original information found.
5. Normalize values only when a documented standard applies.
6. Compare findings across multiple sources.
7. Separate legal entities, parent companies, brands, services, and business units.
8. Record discrepancies, missing information, and limitations.
9. Assign verification statuses and confidence levels.
10. Present findings in a clear, traceable, and decision-ready format.

## Source Standards

Sources are evaluated for:

- **Authority:** Who published, filed, or maintains the information?
- **Accuracy:** Can the information be independently confirmed?
- **Currency:** Is the information current enough for the research purpose?
- **Relevance:** Does the source directly support the required data point?
- **Transparency:** Is the source’s ownership, evidence, or purpose clear?
- **Consistency:** Does the information agree with other authoritative sources?

Government records, regulatory filings, official organizational websites, investor-relations resources, and official guidance are prioritized when appropriate.

Directories, collaborative reference sites, social media, forums, and user-generated content are treated as supporting sources or research leads unless independently verified.

## Verification Standards

Research decisions may use the following statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified | Supported by authoritative evidence without significant qualification |
| Verified with normalization | Supported by the source, with formatting standardized for structured use |
| Verified with context | Supported by the source, but the purpose or relationship requires explanation |
| Partially Verified | Some elements are supported, but one or more details remain uncertain |
| Unable to Verify | Available sources do not provide enough reliable evidence |
| Conflicting | Authoritative sources provide materially different values that remain unresolved |

Confidence levels are assigned as High, Moderate, or Low based on source authority, currency, corroboration, and unresolved risk.
