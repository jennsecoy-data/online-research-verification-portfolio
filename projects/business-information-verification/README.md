## Research Method

1. Define the business information requiring verification.
2. Identify the best authoritative source for each field.
3. Preserve the information exactly as found.
4. Normalize values only when a documented standard applies.
5. Separate legal entities, parent companies, brands, services, and business units.
6. Compare findings across sources.
7. Document discrepancies and contextual differences.
8. Assign verification statuses and confidence levels.
9. Record source URLs and access dates.
10. Present findings in a traceable and database-ready format.

## Verification Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified | Supported by authoritative evidence without significant qualification |
| Verified with normalization | Supported by the source, with formatting standardized for structured use |
| Verified with context | Supported by the source, but its purpose or relationship requires explanation |
| Partially Verified | Some elements are supported, but one or more details remain uncertain |
| Unable to Verify | Available sources do not provide enough reliable evidence |
| Conflicting | Authoritative sources provide materially different values that remain unresolved |

## Data-Quality Principles

### Preserve Source Values

The original information remains documented even when a normalized value is recommended.

### Match Fields to Appropriate Sources

Different sources may be authoritative for different information:

- Regulatory filings for legal and registrant details
- Government registries for filing records
- Official websites for products and services
- Customer-support pages for customer contact information
- Investor-relations pages for public-company reporting

### Label Information by Purpose

Telephone numbers, email addresses, and physical locations should be labeled according to their intended purpose.

Examples include:

- Customer-service phone
- Corporate phone
- SEC registrant phone
- Investor-relations contact
- Principal executive office
- Corporate headquarters
- Registered-agent address

### Preserve Organizational Relationships

Parent companies, subsidiaries, brands, services, and operating segments should not be treated as interchangeable.
