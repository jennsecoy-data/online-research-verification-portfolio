## Executive Summary

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission requires importers of regulated consumer products to electronically submit Certificate of Compliance information through U.S. Customs and Border Protection’s Automated Commercial Environment.

The general eFiling requirements took effect on July 8, 2026. Requirements for regulated merchandise withdrawn from a Foreign Trade Zone for consumption or warehousing take effect on January 8, 2027.

The rule changes how required certificate information is submitted. It does not independently determine which products require testing or certification.

Importers remain responsible for determining whether a finished product is subject to a CPSC rule, ban, standard, or regulation and for ensuring that the appropriate certification information is accurate and available.

Importers can generally submit certificate information using:

1. A Full Partner Government Agency Message Set containing all required certificate data.
2. A Reference Partner Government Agency Message Set that points to certificate information stored in the CPSC Product Registry.

There is no general de minimis shipment exemption when a product otherwise requires CPSC certification.

## Who Must Comply

The eFiling requirement generally applies to importers of finished consumer products that:

- Are subject to a CPSC rule, ban, standard, or regulation
- Require a Children’s Product Certificate or General Certificate of Conformity
- Are imported for consumption or warehousing
- Are distributed in U.S. commerce

The importer responsible for certification is generally the Importer of Record eligible to make entry for the finished product.

Depending on the transaction, the responsible party may be an owner, purchaser, consignee, or authorized customs broker.

Trade partners may assist with data collection or certification tasks when authorized, but the importer remains ultimately responsible for product certification.

## Domestic Manufacturing Distinction

Domestic manufacturers are subject to updated Certificate of Compliance content requirements beginning July 8, 2026.

However, CPSC eFiling through CBP applies to imported regulated consumer products. Domestically manufactured products are not submitted through the import eFiling process.

This distinction should be documented clearly to avoid applying an import-entry workflow to domestic production.

## Required Certificate Data Elements

CPSC identifies seven required Certificate of Compliance data elements.

| Data element | Information required |
|---|---|
| Product ID | An identifier for the finished product |
| Citation codes | Each applicable consumer product safety rule, ban, standard, or regulation |
| Manufacture date | Date the finished product was manufactured |
| Manufacture place | Name, full address, and contact information for the manufacturing party |
| Product test date | Date the finished product was most recently tested for compliance |
| Testing laboratory | Name, full address, and contact information for the testing entity or entities |
| Point of contact | Contact information for the party maintaining the product’s test records |

Acceptable Product ID types may include:

- GTIN
- SKU
- UPC
- Model number
- Serial number
- Registered number
- Alternate ID

The selected identifier should accurately identify the individual product and correspond to the identifier type entered.

## Filing Options

### Full PGA Message Set

Under the Full Partner Government Agency Message Set method:

- All seven required certificate data elements are transmitted through CBP’s Automated Commercial Environment.
- The certificate data is submitted at the time of entry.
- This method may be practical for importers with a limited number of regulated products or products that are not imported repeatedly.

### Reference PGA Message Set

Under the Reference Partner Government Agency Message Set method:

- Certificate information is stored in the CPSC Product Registry.
- The importer supplies certificate identifiers through the CBP entry process.
- The identifiers point to the applicable certificate stored in the Product Registry.
- This method may be useful when the same regulated products and certificates are used repeatedly.

The CPSC Product Registry is a separate certificate-data repository. It does not directly transmit information to CBP’s Automated Commercial Environment.

Importers must still ensure that the appropriate reference information is communicated through the customs-entry process.

## Product Registry Certificate Identifiers

A Reference PGA Message Set uses three identifiers to locate the appropriate certificate:

| Identifier | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Certifier ID | Identifies the importer or certifying organization associated with the certificate |
| Product ID | Identifies the product being certified |
| Version ID | Identifies the specific version of the product certificate |

A new certificate version may be needed when information changes, including:

- New compliance testing
- A material product change
- A manufacturing-process change
- A component-source change
- A different manufacturing facility
- A different testing laboratory
- A correction to previously certified information

## Process Overview

1. Identify the imported finished product.
2. Determine whether the product is subject to a CPSC rule, ban, standard, or regulation.
3. Determine whether a Children’s Product Certificate or General Certificate of Conformity is required.
4. Identify all applicable citation codes.
5. Gather the seven required certificate data elements.
6. Verify manufacturing and testing information.
7. Confirm which party is responsible for certification.
8. Select the Full PGA or Reference PGA filing method.
9. Coordinate certificate information with brokers and other trade partners.
10. Submit the required information through the CBP entry process.
11. Maintain supporting test reports and certification records.
12. Update certificate versions when relevant product or testing information changes.

## De Minimis Shipments

There is no general de minimis exemption from CPSC eFiling.

When an imported product requires certification, its certificate information must be eFiled regardless of the shipment’s value.

Direct-to-consumer and marketplace transactions may still qualify as commercial transactions. The responsible importer should not assume that low shipment value removes the certification requirement.

## Foreign Trade Zones

For regulated consumer products entered into a Foreign Trade Zone and later withdrawn for consumption or warehousing, the applicable implementation date is January 8, 2027.

Certificate information must be submitted as part of the applicable CBP entry process when the merchandise is withdrawn from the Foreign Trade Zone.

Organizations using Foreign Trade Zones should maintain a separate implementation schedule so the later effective date is not confused with a permanent exemption.

## Exceptions and Limitations

Possible exclusions or special situations may include:

- Products that are not subject to a CPSC certification requirement
- Noncommercial transfers between consumers
- Products imported only as samples and not intended for consumer distribution or use
- Used products manufactured before the applicable CPSC rule became effective
- Products covered by a valid testing exclusion
- Situations where a Disclaim message may be appropriate

These situations require careful review. An exemption from one requirement should not be assumed to remove unrelated customs, safety, labeling, or agency obligations.

## Compliance Risks

| Risk | Possible impact | Recommended control |
|---|---|---|
| Incorrect determination that a product is unregulated | Missing certificate data or noncompliant import | Maintain a documented product-applicability review |
| Missing citation codes | Incomplete or inaccurate certificate | Use current CPSC regulatory guidance and product-specific review |
| Incorrect Product ID | Certificate cannot be matched reliably to the product | Use identifiers found on packaging, invoices, or packing records |
| Inaccurate manufacturer or laboratory information | Certificate reliability concerns | Validate names, addresses, and contact information before filing |
| Outdated certificate version | Entry references obsolete testing or manufacturing data | Establish version-control procedures |
| Miscommunication with customs broker | Incorrect or missing entry data | Define responsibilities and transfer requirements in writing |
| Assuming de minimis shipments are exempt | Unfiled certificate information | Apply product-certification rules regardless of shipment value |
| Treating the Product Registry as connected directly to ACE | Missing Reference PGA submission | Ensure certificate identifiers are transmitted through the entry process |
| Applying import eFiling to domestic products | Incorrect workflow | Separate domestic certificate updates from imported-product eFiling |
| Failure to retain test records | Inability to support certification | Maintain organized and accessible compliance documentation |

## Recommended Operational Controls

Organizations supporting CPSC eFiling should consider maintaining:

- A regulated-product inventory
- A product-to-citation-code reference table
- A certificate-data checklist
- Manufacturer and laboratory contact records
- Product and certificate version controls
- Testing and certificate-expiration tracking
- A broker communication procedure
- Data-quality validation before entry
- Exception and discrepancy logs
- Access controls for the Product Registry
- A process for monitoring CPSC guidance updates

## Final Assessment

**Overall confidence:** High

**Conclusion:**

CPSC eFiling is now an active operational requirement for most imported regulated consumer products requiring Certificates of Compliance.

Successful implementation depends on accurate product classification, complete certificate information, controlled data transfer, clear assignment of importer and trade-partner responsibilities, and reliable record maintenance.

The requirement should be treated as a data-quality and compliance workflow rather than as a one-time filing task.

**Recommended next steps:**

1. Inventory imported consumer products.
2. Identify which products require certification.
3. Map products to applicable CPSC citation codes.
4. Collect and validate the seven required certificate data elements.
5. Decide whether each product will use a Full or Reference PGA Message Set.
6. Establish broker and trade-partner communication procedures.
7. Implement certificate version control.
8. Monitor CPSC guidance and Foreign Trade Zone deadlines.
