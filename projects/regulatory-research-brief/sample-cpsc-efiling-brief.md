## Executive Summary

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission requires importers of consumer products subject to CPSC certification requirements to electronically submit Certificate of Compliance information through U.S. Customs and Border Protection’s Automated Commercial Environment.

The general eFiling requirements took effect on July 8, 2026. Requirements for covered merchandise entered from a Foreign Trade Zone for consumption or warehousing take effect on January 8, 2027.

The rule changes how required certificate information is submitted. It does not independently determine which products require testing or certification.

Importers remain responsible for:

- Determining whether a finished product is subject to a CPSC rule, ban, standard, or regulation
- Determining whether a Children’s Product Certificate or General Certificate of Conformity is required
- Identifying every applicable citation
- Maintaining accurate testing and manufacturing information
- Identifying any testing exclusion relied upon
- Ensuring certificate information is submitted correctly during the entry process

Importers can generally submit certificate information using:

1. A Full Partner Government Agency Message Set containing the required certificate data.
2. A Reference Partner Government Agency Message Set that points to certificate information stored in the CPSC Product Registry.

CPSC identifies seven core certificate data elements. When a product requires certification and a testing exclusion applies, the applicable testing-exclusion code must also be provided.

There is no general de minimis shipment exemption when a product otherwise requires CPSC certification.

## Who Must Comply

The eFiling requirement generally applies to importers of finished consumer products that:

- Are subject to a CPSC rule, ban, standard, or regulation
- Require a Children’s Product Certificate or General Certificate of Conformity
- Are imported for consumption or warehousing
- Are distributed in U.S. commerce

The importer responsible for certification is generally the Importer of Record eligible to make entry for the finished product.

Depending on the transaction, the responsible party may be an owner, purchaser, consignee, or an authorized customs broker acting on behalf of the responsible importer.

Trade partners may assist with:

- Data collection
- Product classification
- Broker communication
- Certificate preparation
- Product Registry entry
- PGA Message Set transmission

However, using a broker or other trade partner does not remove the importer’s responsibility for accurate certification.

## Domestic Manufacturing Distinction

Domestic manufacturers are subject to updated Certificate of Compliance content requirements beginning July 8, 2026.

However, CPSC eFiling through CBP applies to imported products.

Domestically manufactured products are not submitted through the import eFiling process, although their manufacturers must comply with the revised certificate-content requirements.

This distinction should be documented clearly to avoid applying an import-entry workflow to domestic production.

## Seven Core Certificate Data Elements

CPSC identifies seven core Certificate of Compliance data elements.

| Data element | Information required |
|---|---|
| Product ID | An identifier for the finished product |
| Citation codes | Each applicable consumer product safety rule, ban, standard, or regulation |
| Manufacture date | Date the finished product was manufactured |
| Manufacture place | Name, full address, and contact information for the manufacturing party |
| Product test date | Date the finished product was most recently tested for compliance |
| Testing laboratory | Name, full address, and contact information for the testing entity or entities |
| Point of contact | Contact information for the party maintaining the product’s test records |

Acceptable Product ID types may include:

- GTIN
- SKU
- UPC
- Model number
- Serial number
- Registered number
- Alternate ID

The selected identifier should accurately identify the individual finished product and correspond to the identifier type entered.

## Testing-Exclusion Codes

The seven core data elements do not eliminate the need to identify an applicable testing exclusion.

When:

1. The product requires certification, and
2. The certificate relies on a testing exclusion,

the applicable testing-exclusion code must be included with the product certificate.

Testing exclusions should not be treated as informal notes. The exclusion used should be documented through the applicable CPSC code and supported by records explaining why it applies.

A testing exclusion may affect how testing information is completed, but it does not automatically eliminate the product’s certification requirement.

Recommended controls include:

- Record the applicable citation code.
- Record the testing-exclusion code separately.
- Retain evidence supporting the exclusion.
- Confirm that the code applies to the specific product and rule.
- Reevaluate the exclusion after product, material, manufacturing, or regulatory changes.

## Testing Laboratory Information

Certificates must identify the testing used to support the certification.

When multiple laboratories or testing sources support compliance:

- Each relevant laboratory should be identified.
- The laboratory should be associated with the applicable rule, ban, standard, or regulation it tested.
- Component-part testing used to support finished-product certification should be documented appropriately.
- Testing dates and laboratory information should remain traceable to supporting reports.

Optional fields such as test URLs, report keys, or report identifiers may support record traceability even when they are not mandatory.

## Filing Options

### Full PGA Message Set

Under the Full Partner Government Agency Message Set method:

- The required certificate information is transmitted through CBP’s Automated Commercial Environment.
- The certificate data is submitted as part of the entry process.
- The seven core certificate elements must be provided.
- Any applicable testing-exclusion code must also be supplied.
- This method may be practical for importers with a limited number of regulated products or products that are not imported repeatedly.

### Reference PGA Message Set

Under the Reference Partner Government Agency Message Set method:

- Certificate information is stored in the CPSC Product Registry.
- The importer supplies certificate identifiers through the CBP entry process.
- The identifiers point to the applicable certificate stored in the Product Registry.
- Applicable testing-exclusion information is maintained with the certificate data.
- This method may be useful when the same regulated products and certificates are used repeatedly.

The CPSC Product Registry is a certificate-data repository. It does not replace the need to submit the required certificate identifiers through the customs-entry process.

Importers must ensure that the correct identifiers are communicated to their broker or other trade partner.

## Product Registry Certificate Identifiers

A Reference PGA Message Set uses three identifiers to locate the appropriate certificate.

| Identifier | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Certifier ID | Identifies the importer or certifying organization associated with the certificate |
| Product ID | Identifies the product being certified |
| Version ID | Identifies the specific version of the product certificate |

All three identifiers must point to the intended certificate version.

A mismatch could cause:

- An incorrect certificate to be referenced
- Obsolete information to be submitted
- Entry delays
- Data validation problems
- Difficulty supporting the certification during review

## Certificate Version Control

A new certificate version may be needed when information changes, including:

- New compliance testing
- A material product change
- A manufacturing-process change
- A component-source change
- A different manufacturing facility
- A different testing laboratory
- A change in applicable citation codes
- A change in testing-exclusion status
- A correction to previously certified information
- A change to the person maintaining test records

Organizations should document:

- What changed
- Why a new version was created
- When the new version became effective
- Which previous version it replaced
- Which shipments or production periods use each version

## Process Overview

1. Identify the imported finished product.
2. Determine whether the product is subject to a CPSC rule, ban, standard, or regulation.
3. Determine whether a Children’s Product Certificate or General Certificate of Conformity is required.
4. Identify every applicable citation code.
5. Determine whether any testing exclusion applies.
6. Record the applicable testing-exclusion code when required.
7. Gather the seven core certificate data elements.
8. Verify manufacturing and testing information.
9. Associate testing laboratories with the requirements they tested.
10. Confirm which party is responsible for certification.
11. Select the Full PGA or Reference PGA filing method.
12. Coordinate certificate information with brokers and other trade partners.
13. Submit the required information through the CBP entry process.
14. Maintain supporting test reports and certification records.
15. Update certificate versions when product, manufacturing, testing, or regulatory information changes.

## De Minimis Shipments

There is no general de minimis exemption from CPSC eFiling.

When an imported product requires certification, its certificate information must be eFiled regardless of the shipment’s value.

Importers should not assume that:

- A low-value shipment is automatically exempt.
- Direct-to-consumer shipping removes the requirement.
- Marketplace fulfillment transfers responsibility away from the importer.
- A small quantity removes product-certification obligations.

The certification analysis should focus on the product and transaction, not only on shipment value.

## Foreign Trade Zones

For regulated consumer products entered into a Foreign Trade Zone and later withdrawn for consumption or warehousing, the applicable implementation date is January 8, 2027.

Certificate information must be submitted through the applicable CBP entry process when the covered merchandise is withdrawn from the Foreign Trade Zone.

Organizations using Foreign Trade Zones should maintain a separate implementation schedule so the later effective date is not confused with a permanent exemption.

## Possible Exclusions and Special Situations

Situations requiring additional review may include:

- Products that are not subject to a CPSC certification requirement
- Used products manufactured before the applicable CPSC rule became effective
- Noncommercial consumer-to-consumer transfers
- Products imported only as samples and not intended for consumer distribution or use
- Products relying on a valid testing exclusion
- Entries for which a Disclaim message may be appropriate
- Products subject to requirements administered by more than one agency
- Products with incomplete manufacturing or testing histories

These situations require careful review.

An exemption or exclusion from one requirement should not be assumed to remove:

- Other CPSC obligations
- Customs requirements
- Labeling requirements
- Record-retention requirements
- Requirements enforced by another agency
- State or local obligations

## Disclaim PGA Message Sets

CPSC does not require importers to transmit a Disclaim PGA Message Set in every situation where certification is not required.

However, CPSC may encourage use of an appropriate Disclaim code because it can help explain why certificate data is not being submitted.

A Disclaim should not be used as a substitute for a proper certification analysis.

The importer should retain documentation supporting:

- Why certification was not required
- Which Disclaim code was selected
- Which product and shipment the decision applied to
- Who approved the determination
- Which guidance or rule supported the decision

## Compliance Risks

| Risk | Possible impact | Recommended control |
|---|---|---|
| Incorrect determination that a product is unregulated | Missing certificate data or noncompliant import | Maintain a documented product-applicability review |
| Missing citation codes | Incomplete or inaccurate certificate | Use current CPSC guidance and product-specific review |
| Missing testing-exclusion code | Incomplete certificate when an exclusion is relied upon | Maintain a separate exclusion-code field and supporting documentation |
| Unsupported testing exclusion | Certification cannot be substantiated | Retain evidence showing why the exclusion applies |
| Incorrect Product ID | Certificate cannot be matched reliably to the product | Use identifiers found on packaging, invoices, or packing records |
| Inaccurate manufacturer information | Certificate reliability concerns | Validate names, addresses, and contact information before filing |
| Inaccurate laboratory information | Testing cannot be traced or linked properly | Validate laboratories and associate them with the applicable citations |
| Outdated certificate version | Entry references obsolete testing or manufacturing data | Establish formal version-control procedures |
| Miscommunication with customs broker | Incorrect or missing entry data | Define responsibilities and transfer requirements in writing |
| Assuming de minimis shipments are exempt | Unfiled certificate information | Apply certification requirements regardless of shipment value |
| Treating the Product Registry as a substitute for PGA transmission | Missing Reference PGA submission | Ensure identifiers are transmitted through the entry process |
| Applying import eFiling to domestic products | Incorrect workflow | Separate domestic certificate updates from imported-product eFiling |
| Failure to retain test records | Inability to support certification | Maintain organized and accessible compliance documentation |
| Incorrect Disclaim code | Entry data does not reflect the product’s actual status | Require documented review before using a Disclaim |

## Final Assessment

**Overall confidence:** High

**Conclusion:**

CPSC eFiling is now an active operational requirement for most imported consumer products requiring Certificates of Compliance.

Successful implementation depends on:

- Accurate product-applicability review
- Complete certificate information
- Correct citation codes
- Identification of testing exclusions when applicable
- Accurate manufacturing and laboratory information
- Controlled data transfer
- Clear assignment of importer and trade-partner responsibilities
- Reliable certificate version control
- Organized record maintenance

The requirement should be treated as a continuing data-quality and compliance workflow rather than as a one-time filing task.

**Recommended next steps:**

1. Inventory imported consumer products.
2. Identify which products require certification.
3. Map products to every applicable CPSC citation code.
4. Identify and document any testing exclusions.
5. Record testing-exclusion codes when applicable.
6. Collect and validate the seven core certificate data elements.
7. Associate testing laboratories with the rules they tested.
8. Decide whether each product will use a Full or Reference PGA Message Set.
9. Establish broker and trade-partner communication procedures.
10. Implement certificate version control.
11. Maintain testing, manufacturing, exclusion, and entry records.
12. Monitor CPSC guidance and Foreign Trade Zone deadlines.
